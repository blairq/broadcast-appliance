Translation takes a **text output** (the one from a “Speech to text” card: “Orador”) and publishes another one with the same thing in another language (“Orador · en”). It runs on the machine, with no network, using OPUS-MT models (0.1 to 0.6 s per sentence). Spanish <-> English and Spanish <-> Portuguese are direct; French, German, Russian and Chinese go through English.

## Recipe: English captions and English voice on track 2
1. Speech to text of the speaker (help {"topic": "audio_rack"}): an output “Orador”, continuous mode.
2. `traduccion_agregar {"desde": "Orador", "a": "en"}`. If it says `faltan`, `traduccion_bajar {"a": "en"}` and wait (modelo_local_estado shows the progress). The pieces of the transcription are joined until a sentence is complete (or 0.6 s with no more text: `espera_ms` in traduccion_ajustar) before translating; if the speaker runs on without a period, after `maximo_ms` (8 s) whatever there is gets translated. It shows up as a “Translate” card in the source's audio rack (next to its speech to text; it takes no cables); the operator sees it and adjusts it there.
2b. Captions: in the scene, a **“Captions”** box (`crear_fuente` type `ba_subtitulos`, settings `{"flujo": "Orador · en"}`; at the bottom and full width, e.g. layout {x: 0.08, y: 0.78, w: 0.84, h: 0.16}). It splits into lines (`caracteres`, 42), shows `lineas` (2) at a time, each block for as long as it takes to read it (`cps`, 17 characters per second; `minimo` 1.5 s), with a background behind each line, and clears itself (`borrar`). For original and translation at the same time, two boxes. To test without speaking: `texto_publicar {"flujo": "Orador", "texto": "..."}`.
3. `texto_a_voz {"fuente": "Traduccion", "voz": "csukuangfj/vits-piper-en_US-amy-medium", "lee": "Orador · en"}` and in the Matrix route that source to track 2.
4. `traducciones` shows the last translated sentence and how long it took.
5. Several speakers at once, or the text is slow: the transcription can use Nemotron (streaming) instead of Parakeet: `voz_reconocedores {"consumidor": "transcripcion", "motor": "nemotron"}` (help {"topic": "agentes"}).

## The glossary (what must not be mistranslated)
Before translating, each known term is replaced by its ready-made translation. Without it, “cuarto intermedio” comes out as “middle room” and “no es moco de pavo” as “turkey mucus”. It comes with three groups: “general” (booth), “asamblea” (sessions, motions, “sobre tablas”, caucuses, committees) and “localidades” (places). **Before an event, add its terms**: names of committees, programs, named laws, positions, institutions.
- `glosario_ver {"buscar"?}`: what there is, and `archivo`: the operator's JSON (Datos/Config/glosario-traduccion.json). It can be edited directly: each term `{"es": ["forma", "otra forma"], "en": "...", "pt": "...", "fr": "...", "de": "...", "ru": "...", "zh": "...", "grupo": "..."}`; it overrides the factory one by its first form.
- `glosario_agregar {"es": [...], "en": "...", ...}`, `glosario_quitar {"es"}` or `{"grupo", "activo": false}`.
- Test with `traducir {"texto", "a"}` before going on air.
- Rules: several forms in `es` (singular, plural, feminine); the translation must fit the sentence (if the term is “solicito pasar a cuarto intermedio”, the translation should be the whole sentence); in Russian and Chinese the term is applied when going through English, so take care of the English form (common words: “parliamentary group”, not “caucus”).
- The models translate word for word what they don't know: a new idiom, or a proper name that can be read as a common word (Esquel, Gaiman…), goes in the glossary with its form in each language.
