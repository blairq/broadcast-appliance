# Capacity: how many remote guests

How many guests can join the conference over WebRTC at the same time, measured on real
hardware, and what runs out first. The full measurements (in Spanish) are in
[`docs/hardware/`](../hardware/README.md), one page per machine.

| Machine | System | Guests (measured) | What limits first |
|---|---|---|---|
| AMD Ryzen 9 5900X (12 cores / 24 threads), AMD Radeon RX 5700 XT (8 GB), 32 GB DDR4 | Windows 11 | **~160 with headroom; 200 join and run** (thumbnails slower) | the GPU's video decode engine (from ~120), then the gateway's network threads (at 200) |
| Apple MacBook Pro, M1 Pro (8 cores: 6 performance + 2 efficiency, 14-core GPU), 32 GB unified memory | macOS 26 | **40 with headroom** — the highest step measured; the ceiling was not measured | not reached (see below) |

The guest limit in the program is **512** (it was 64 until November 2026); below that, the
machine decides. To measure your own machine, use **Configuration → System → Capacity**: it
ramps up test guests step by step and tells you how many it holds with headroom and what
limits it ([CAPACIDAD.md](../funciones/CAPACIDAD.md), in Spanish).

## Where the cost is

- **Each guest sends simulcast** (180p at 15 fps, 360p, and 720p at 30 fps), but the browser
  only encodes and sends the layer the room asks for: grid thumbnails get the low layer, large
  cells the medium one (360p), and **at most 4 guests are in high definition** at once (the
  speaker first). A guest that nobody sees (not on air, not in a grid cell, not on any monitor
  or preview) is paused and not received at all.
- **Each guest receives a single return**: the program video, encoded **once** per layer and
  forwarded to everyone (like an SFU), plus their own **mix-minus** audio (the program without
  their own voice), whose Opus encoding is also shared among guests who hear the same thing.
- So the work is on the appliance: **decoding every camera that is shown**, the people grid,
  the audio mix and the network. Outbound traffic is about **1.7 Mbps per guest** (70 Mbps with
  40, 106 Mbps with 60).
- **The number depends on the graphics card**: guest video is H.264 decoded and encoded in
  hardware (Direct3D 11 / AMD on Windows, VideoToolbox on macOS). On the Windows PC the
  GPU's video engine is what fills up first; a card with more decode throughput moves the
  ceiling. Without a hardware H.264 decoder the gateway uses software codecs (VP8), which cost more CPU.

## Test conditions

- Guests are real WebRTC peers generated by **hinchada** (our load tester): recorded 720p
  video in simulcast with each layer labelled («720p», «360p», «180p»), synthetic voice,
  **one guest speaking**, H.264 constrained baseline. It decodes every return it receives, as
  a browser would.
- The people grid is on air (program), with thumbnails in the low layer and the speaker in
  720p.
- **Windows**: the appliance runs on the PC; the guests come from another machine over the
  LAN (1 Gbps wired on the PC), through the appliance's own **TURN relay** (10 % of them over
  TCP), the same path guests from the internet use. **Mac**: app and load generator on the
  same machine, direct path (numbers are pessimistic: the generator's load is included).
- Each run is **2 minutes at most**: guests join (10 per second for the ladder; the factory
  default is 2 per second, see *staggered entry* below), the room settles, and 45-60 s of
  steady state are measured.

## Windows PC (Ryzen 9 5900X + RX 5700 XT)

Measured on 2026-11-03 with everything integrated. CPU is in **% of one core** (100 % = one
full thread; the machine has 24).

| Guests | ba-app (engine + UI) | ba-gateway | Grid thumbnails | Guest audio age | Longest UI stall | GPU video engine |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| 40 | 102 % | 185 % | 15.2 fps | 15 ms | 0.2 s | 48 % |
| 80 | 178 % | 348 % | 15.0 fps | 15 ms | 0.9 s | 82 % |
| 120 | 264 % | 493 % | 13.2 fps | 12 ms | 0.7 s | **96-99 %** |
| 160 | 378 % | 615 % | 9.9 fps | 13 ms | 1.3 s | 98-99 % |
| 200 | 496 % | 707 % | 7.5 fps | 13 ms | 2.7 s while joining* | 98-99 % |

\* After the UI fixes of the same night, a walk through every page with 200 guests joining
had no stall longer than 2 s, except the **Latencias** (latency) page (4.6-5.0 s when it first shows
hundreds of cards).

- In every step: the grid mosaic stays at 30 fps, audio stays at 12-15 ms, no late audio
  blocks, and every guest gets in (200 in 103 s at 10 per second). Memory: ba-app reaches
  8.4 GB with 200; at least 10 GB stay free.
- **Before the multi-core gateway** (2026-11-02) the ceiling was **~80**: with 80 the UI froze
  6-8 s when everyone left at once, and with 120 the single audio playout thread was full.
  With 40 guests, the gateway went from **220 % to 169 %** of a core, from **647 to 234
  threads** and from **45,000 to 23,700 context switches per second**.
- **What fills up**: the GPU video decode engine from ~120 (thumbnails drop below 15 fps),
  then the gateway's two network threads (97-98 % each with 200). At 160-200 the load
  generator itself was at its limit, so guest-side freezes in those steps are partly the
  generator's. 256-512 were not measured (they need two generating machines).
- After these measurements the number of gateway network threads became automatic (one per
  3 physical cores: 4 on this PC instead of the 2 measured). That should move the 200-guest
  network limit, but it **has not been measured yet**.
- Over the internet through a free Pinggy tunnel, the tunnel caps at ~7-8 Mbps: about **5
  guests** with video. More need a paid tunnel or another route.

## Mac (M1 Pro)

Measured on 2026-11-02, before the multi-core gateway, with H.264 through VideoToolbox:

| Guests | 10 | 20 | 30 | 40 |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| ba-app | 50 % | 64 % | 75 % | 101 % |
| ba-gateway | 87 % | 62 % | 85 % | 104 % |
| Load generator (same machine) | 30 % | 46 % | 66 % | 92 % |
| Whole machine (8 cores, generator included) | 53 % | 54 % | 65 % | 75 % |
| Grid: mosaic / cells | 30 / 29.5 fps (all HD) | 30 / 15 | 30 / 15 | 30 / 15 |
| Guest audio age | 11 ms | 14 ms | 12 ms | 13 ms |

40 guests run without degradation. **Higher steps were not measured on the Mac**, nor was
it re-measured after the multi-core gateway: with the generator on the same machine it was
already using almost a full core at 40, so going further needs the guests generated from
another machine. No ceiling is claimed for the Mac.

## Recent work behind these numbers

- **Multi-core gateway**: network and worker thread layers instead of threads per guest; the
  TURN relay and the WebRTC network share threads and hand packets over in memory; libwebrtc's
  task queues share two small thread pools; the audio playout is split over several threads;
  hardware H.264 (Direct3D 11 on Windows, VideoToolbox on macOS).
- **Video on demand**: guests nobody sees are paused; the 360p middle layer for large cells;
  lower frame rate for thumbnails when the GPU decoder is saturated.
- **Staggered entry** (`room.entradaPorSegundo`, 2 per second by default): a burst of
  guests joins in order instead of all at once.
- **A decoupled UI**: the interface never waits for the engine, the gateway, the disk or the
  network; a development-only *coupling detector* finds the places where it still does.
